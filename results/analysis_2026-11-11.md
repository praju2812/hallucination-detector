### Interpretation (A)
- The pooled lengths are strongly right-skewed (skew 2.4) because they mix short HaluEval rows (mean 122) with long
  Summary rows (mean 869), so a single mean+2SD describes the mixture rather than any one source.
- On the pooled training data, mean+2SD is 1021 tokens (94.8th percentile), next to the current 1024 window, which
  truncates 5.1% of training rows; all of them are RAGTruth Summary (29.6% of Summary training rows), and no HaluEval or
  RAGTruth QA row exceeds 1024.
- Judged by the worst group (Summary), 95% coverage needs a 2048 window (97.4% covered) and 97.5% coverage needs 2304;
  the pooled data alone would need 1280 and 1536. At 2048 the proxy cost is 1.07 to 1.35 times the 1024 cost on the
  pooled training data.
- Within Summary test rows, G's AUROC is 0.662 +/- 0.036 on truncated rows and 0.652 +/- 0.070 on untruncated rows;
  PR-AUC is 0.420 +/- 0.027 vs 0.349 +/- 0.090, with prevalence 0.275 vs 0.206, so truncated rows did not score lower
  in these three runs.
- The Phase 2 report's truncated vs untruncated gap (seed 42: AUROC 0.696 vs 0.823) compares 269 Summary rows with
  1,531 untruncated RAGTruth rows, 900 of which are QA; within Summary the seed 42 figures are 0.696 vs 0.732.

### Interpretation (A7, clinical)
- Clinical lengths are less skewed than the pooled general training data (skew 0.74 vs 2.42), and their mean+2SD
  values (566 to 920 tokens in train) fall at the 96.2nd to 99.4th percentile.
- At 1024, 0.5% of clinical training rows and 0.2% of clean test rows are truncated, all of them RAGBench PubMedQA
  (1.0% of its train rows, 0.8% of its clean test rows); no CovidQA or MedHallu row exceeds 1024.
- A 1024 window meets both the 95% and the 97.5% coverage target for every clinical source (worst: PubMedQA, 99.0%
  covered in train), so across all training data the window is set by RAGTruth Summary, not by the clinical data.

### Caveats (A)
- A7 uses `build()` run in memory at ef25372; if the build changes before `data/clinical` is written, A7 must be rerun.
- A3 uses only the 1024 window (the window the runs used), three runs, and 269 truncated rows with 74 positives.
  PR-AUC depends on prevalence, which differs between the two subsets.
- The compute figures are proxies from token counts. They ignore padding to the longest row in each batch (which raises
  the real cost when long Summary rows share a batch with short rows) and kernel effects.
- GPU memory at windows above 1024 can only be confirmed by a real run. Prajakta reports that 1024 used 7.7 of 15 GB on a
  Kaggle T4 [CHECK: not taken from a run file read for this report].
- The comment in `configs/base.yaml` (p95 = 1006) predates the query field; with the query, train p95 is 1035.

= clinical train BEFORE any training-side removal (RAGBench PubMedQA train 19,600 rows, RAGBench CovidQA train 1,252,
pqa_artificial 90/10 train portion 16,262) + `data/unified_v2/train.jsonl` (25,008). Keys = `overlap_keys(row, min_words)`
at ef25372: the normalised question, and each passage with at least `min_words` normalised words. Variants: min_words=0
(every non-empty passage) and min_words=8 (the build default). RAGTruth Summary rows are left out of question comparisons
(their query is the constant "Summarize the following news"). Removal is per row; removing whole groups instead gives the

### Interpretation (D)
- In HaluEval, question-answer overlap separates the labels strongly (AUROC 0.915 Jaccard, 0.904 TF-IDF), but answer
  length alone separates them more (0.972; hallucinated answers average 11.0 words, faithful ones 2.2).
- After stratifying by answer length, the HaluEval question-answer effect shrinks to 0.625 (weighted) and 0.533
  (rank, CI 0.525 to 0.540): a smaller question effect survives, and the context-answer features fall below 0.5
  (0.205 to 0.206 weighted, 0.443 rank).
- In RAGTruth QA, the question-answer features are close to chance before stratifying (0.426, 0.557) and do not survive
  it (0.492 to 0.518; both rank CIs include 0.5), while answer length alone gives 0.709.
- In RAGTruth QA the context-answer effect survives and grows after stratifying (0.314 to 0.343): within an
  answer-length decile, hallucinated answers share fewer words with the context.
- The dropped QA instruction is one constant 59-token text across all 989 sources, so it carries no per-row information.

### Caveats (D)
- Jaccard and TF-IDF cosine are lexical proxies; they do not show what the detector uses.
- TF-IDF is fitted on all splits, test included; this is descriptive, nothing was selected or tuned on it.
- HaluEval rows come in pairs sharing a question and context (one group each); the bootstrap resamples whole groups.
- Most HaluEval deciles are almost all one class (e.g. 77 positives in 3,587 rows; 1,841 in 1,878), so the
  within-decile AUROCs rest on few minority-class rows. The two stratified summaries differ because the pooled-rank
  version also compares rows from different deciles, which moves it toward 0.5 when the label rate differs between deciles.
- Answer length is in whitespace words; deciles are per source over all splits.
